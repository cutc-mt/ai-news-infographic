In this video, I'll cover the Inkling 2 Mini launch, how its looped transformer reuses layers, what it takes to run on a 32 GB Mac, and what OpenRouter charges. Then I'll show how it handled five jobs I'd give a local model: patching Python, calling tools, fixing SQLite queries, writing a log CLI, and building a regex inspector. I ran them on OpenRouter for about 3 cents across all tests and retries, and I'll close with the jobs I'd trust it with and the fixes I'd double-check.

Key Takeaways:

Mini stores 24 layers and runs the middle 12 three times, giving each token 48 layer passes.
Thinking Machines recommends at least 32 GB of unified memory on an Apple Silicon Mac, and Unsloth's 4-bit GGUF weighs 17.6 GB.
Prime Intellect charged 10 cents per million input tokens and 40 cents per million output tokens on OpenRouter, and my 19 requests together cost about 3 cents.
Mini tied Qwen at 20.1 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, and Thinking Machines reports 79.2% on SWE-bench Verified.
Low effort fixed the cache bug in 4 seconds and high took 15.4 seconds, with all 9 checks passing at every effort level.
The patched Python limiter let just 1 of 16 simultaneous requests through, but its own tests still passed when I removed the lock.
Auto mode returned a complete JSON migration plan and passed 8 of 8 checks, while required mode missed two tool calls and scored 5 of 8.
SQLite revenue matched to the cent, but the rolling total dropped January data and the log CLI missed a Unicode case match.
 The single-file regex inspector passed all 11 checks, including live highlighting and recovery from an invalid pattern.
 I'd try Mini at low or medium effort for focused fixes and small utilities on a 32 GB Mac, and give giant one-shot apps to a larger model.
